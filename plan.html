1. Executive summary

Jax Vendor Events (working name) is a free website and weekly email that lists every vendor market, craft fair, pop-up and festival in Northeast Florida (Duval, St. Johns, Clay, Nassau, Baker, Flagler and Putnam counties), with the date, the vendor fee, the application deadline and a direct link to apply. It is built for the small vendors who sell at these markets, and paid for by the organizers and local businesses who want to reach them.

2. Problem and customers

Vendors (the readers)

Small sellers of jewelry, candles, clothing, crafts, art, plants, baked goods and resale who do 2–8 markets a month. Their pain: finding markets that fit, knowing the fee before wasting time, and catching the application deadline. Today that means checking a dozen Facebook groups and pages every week.

Organizers (the main payers)

Churches, schools, breweries, HOAs, cities, nonprofits and independent market producers. Their pain: filling every booth, especially for first-year events and slower months, and reaching vendors outside their own followers. Many already pay for Facebook boosts or Eventeny tools.

Local businesses that sell to vendors (sponsors)

Canopy and display suppliers, card readers, printers, insurance agents, storage, packaging. They want a concentrated audience of small sellers, which is hard to buy on Facebook.

Shoppers (later)

People looking for a market this weekend. Not the first audience, but the same list serves them and can grow traffic later with a “This weekend” page.

3. Product

  • Events directory: every listing with date, city and county, fee, deadline, Checked/Unverified badge with the date we read the organizer's page, and an apply link. Filters by month, county and fee; save events; “My matches” on the device.
  • Weekly email (Mondays): this week + next two weeks, deadlines coming up, free to vend, one tip, one sponsor slot. Issue #1 and #2 drafts are done.
  • City & county signup guide: 12 agencies with how to become a vendor, fees, permits and insurance notes.
  • Profiles: vendor and organizer profiles (moderated), with a $10/week featured spotlight.
  • For organizers: free “List your event” form, “Get featured” request form, advertise page and media kit.
  • Trust pages: About, Terms & Privacy, listing-accuracy policy.
  • Built on: a static site (GitHub Pages, free), FormSubmit forms (free), password gate and no-index while private.

Who could read it (vendors)

  • 11,187 one-person retail businesses and 5,358 independent artists in the 7 counties (Census Nonemployer Statistics 2023). Not all of them sell at markets.
  • The biggest Jacksonville vendor Facebook groups show roughly 14,000–17,000 members (Jacksonville Pop-Up Vendors & Events) and about 11,000 (Jacksonville, Florida Vendors) in search results; Facebook hides exact counts from logged-out visitors, and members overlap.
  • Estimate: about 5,000–15,000 people in NE Florida vend at least occasionally (worked from the group sizes and the Census counts above; not a census figure). Reaching 660 readers in a year (base case) is about 4–13% of that.

How many markets (supply)

  • Our directory holds 192 upcoming listings from 156 organizers and venues across the 7 counties (31 of them repeating weekly or monthly).
  • For comparison, VendorsMap shows 33 upcoming events in Jacksonville, 16 in St. Augustine, 8 in Fernandina Beach, 6 in Orange Park and 5 in Palm Coast (vendorsmap.com city pages, read Nov 1, 2026). FairsAndFestivals.net lists 28 NE Florida events for Nov 2026–May 2027 out of 796 statewide.
  • The state's farmers-market list names only 7 NE Florida markets and says it is incomplete (FDACS); a third-party directory lists 9 in Jacksonville (farmermarkets.app).
  • Seasonality: of the upcoming dates we hold, 83 listings have a date in November and 78 in December, against 31 in February–March (our own data; the spring and summer months are not fully listed yet).

National signals

  • Etsy has about 5.6 million active sellers (Etsy investor page, Dec 31, 2025). Eventeny lists 35,500+ events; VendorsMap 24,400+ events and 69,200+ vendors; FestivalNet 26,000+ events (each site's own page, read Nov 1, 2026).

Takeaway: there are far more markets in NE Florida than any single site lists, and thousands of small sellers looking for them. The gap is a local, checked, weekly list.

Where we win

  • Local and complete: 192 upcoming NE FL listings vs 33 on VendorsMap's Jacksonville page.
  • Checked weekly against each organizer's own page, with the date shown.
  • Vendor-first details: fee, deadline, apply link and city/county signup rules in one place.
  • A Monday email nobody else sends, written by a working local vendor.
  • Cheaper for organizers than Eventeny or VendorsMap paid plans; free basic listings.

Where they win, and what we do about it

  • VendorsMap and Eventeny have application and payment tools; we link to the organizer's own form instead and stay a directory.
  • Facebook groups have the crowd; we post there as a helpful vendor and pull people into the email.
  • VendorsMap's featured spot is $30/month vs our $20/week; ours includes the weekly email to a local list. If organizers push back, a $49 four-week bundle is the fallback price.

Why these prices: a featured week costs less than one vendor booth fee (the median listed fee in our data is $75 across the 55 upcoming listings that publish a fee), so one extra vendor pays for it. The sponsor price is set per issue, not per reader, while the list is small, and steps up at 500 and 1,000 readers. For reference: small newsletters (under 5,000 readers) typically charge a flat $50–$250 per placement (beehiiv blog) or $75–$200 per issue under 3,000 readers (artha.link); one local 4,000-reader newsletter charges $75 per ad (search result, unverified). Our $35 launch rate sits below all of these on purpose.

How people pay: by a payment link emailed after we confirm the request. Luis already has a Square seller account, so Square payment links or invoices need no new account; Stripe Payment Links are the alternative. No card details are ever taken in a form.

Launch offers: first month free for the first 5 organizers who feature an event (in exchange for a quote); the first 4-issue sponsor keeps the $35 rate for 6 months.

9. Operations: the weekly update

Tab runs this every week (Sunday afternoon/evening) once Luis approves the schedule; Luis spends about 5 minutes approving.

  1. Pull new events from the daily event search and the vendor database; add new NE Florida events, drop past ones, merge duplicates.
  2. Re-check the next 3 weeks of listings against each organizer's own page; mark Checked with the date, or Cancelled, or leave Unverified.
  3. Process form submissions (new listings, featured requests, profiles) from the inbox.
  4. Draft the Monday email (this week + next 2 weeks, deadlines, free to vend, one tip, sponsor slot) as a preview page.
  5. Send Luis a 2-line summary + preview link. Nothing publishes and no email goes out without his OK (until he gives a standing OK).
  6. After the send: log readers, opens and clicks; invoice any featured or sponsor spots that ran.

The full routine is ready to schedule as a written instruction file.

10. Legal basics (Florida)

  • Business form: run it under Luis's existing business as a fictitious name (DBA), registered with the Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz). The filing fee is $50 and lasts 5 years; the name must be advertised once in a newspaper in the county (Luis certifies it; no proof is filed). Source: Florida Division of Corporations (dos.fl.gov/sunbiz), read Nov 1, 2026.
  • LLC later, if wanted: A Florida LLC costs $125 to form ($100 filing + $25 registered agent) plus a $138.75 annual report due by May 1 ($538.75 if late). Not needed to start; worth it once revenue is steady. Source: dos.fl.gov/sunbiz LLC fees page.
  • Sales tax: Selling advertising (featured listings, sponsorships) is generally not a taxable service in Florida: DOR's taxable-services guide (GT-800013) does not list advertising, and Rule 12A-1.072(2) exempts advertising services. Luis already has a Florida sales-tax registration for JAY STEEL; confirm with DOR whether anything needs to be added. Anything physical sold (printed goods, merch) would be taxable.
  • Local business tax: Jacksonville/Duval requires a local business tax receipt for most businesses, including one-person home businesses (Oct 1–Sep 30). Luis holds one for JAY STEEL; ask the Tax Collector whether the directory needs its own classification added. Source: taxcollector.jacksonville.gov.
  • Terms & Privacy: drafted on the site (listing accuracy, paid placements labelled, refunds, privacy, unsubscribe). Have it reviewed before launch.
  • Data accuracy disclaimer: on every page footer and in every email: confirm details with the organizer before paying.
  • Email law (CAN-SPAM): the newsletter tool adds an unsubscribe link and a mailing address; use a P.O. box or the tool's address option, never Luis's home address.
  • Content: list facts (date, fee, link) in our own words and link to the source; don't copy organizers' flyers or photos without permission.

Not legal or tax advice. Fees and rules are from the official pages listed in the sources and should be confirmed when filing.
