Vendor guide

Florida vendor registration guide

What jewelry, craft and retail vendors need to sell at markets, pop-ups and festivals anywhere in Florida. The statewide steps come first, then all 67 counties with official links.

Statewide stepsFind your county
General information, not legal or tax advice. Rules and fees change, so always confirm with the county, the city and the Florida Department of Revenue before you sell.

Statewide: every vendor, every county

  1. Register for Florida sales tax. If you sell taxable goods (jewelry, crafts, retail), register with the Florida Department of Revenue before your first event. Registration is online and free. Register with DOR
  2. Get your Annual Resale Certificate. Registered dealers get one. It lets you buy inventory for resale without paying tax on it. Annual Resale Certificate
  3. Collect 6% plus the county surtax where you sell. You collect discretionary sales surtax when the sale happens in (or is delivered into) a county that has one. It only applies to the first $5,000 of each item. At an event, use the rate of the county the event is in (each county card below shows it). Surtax rules · 2026 county rates (DR-15DSS)
  4. File and pay on your schedule. Report sales by county on your return. Ask each organizer whether they collect tax for vendors; if they don't, it's on you. Florida sales & use tax
  5. Register your business name. If you sell under a name other than your legal name, file a fictitious name with Sunbiz. An LLC is optional and also filed there. Fictitious name · Florida LLC
  6. Local Business Tax Receipt (BTR). Counties and cities may levy a local business tax under Chapter 205, Florida Statutes. Some counties have repealed theirs, while most cities still collect one, so check both the county and the city where you're based and where you sell. A 2026 bill to repeal local business taxes statewide (HB 103) died in the Senate. Ch. 205 · HB 103
  7. Event permits. The organizer usually holds the special-event permit from the city or county. Ask whether vendors need anything extra (a tent or fire inspection, or a city vendor permit).

Selling food?

Prepared food at a temporary event needs a DBPR temporary food service event license for each event. DBPR temporary event guide

Packaged and retail foods are regulated by FDACS. Florida's cottage food law lets you sell certain low-risk foods made in your home kitchen without a state license. FDACS cottage foods

Some events also ask for a county health department sign-off, so check with the organizer.
